## Abstract
The present study investigated the effects of an eight-week high-intensity interval training (HIIT) program on maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max) and lactate threshold (LT) in male collegiate football players. Using a randomized controlled design, forty players (age: 20.3 +/- 1.6 years; VO2max: 48.7 +/- 3.2 mL/kg/min) were assigned equally to a HIIT group (HG) or a moderate-intensity continuous training group (CG). The HG performed three weekly sessions of 4 x 4 minutes at 90-95% HRmax separated by three-minute active recovery periods over eight weeks. VO2max was assessed via a maximal incremental treadmill protocol, and lactate threshold was identified at 4 mmol/L blood lactate. Post-intervention, the HG demonstrated significantly greater improvements in VO2max (D = +5.8 mL/kg/min; p &lt; 0.001; d = 2.21) and lactate threshold velocity (D = +1.4 km/h; p &lt; 0.001; d = 1.93) compared to the CG. Heart rate recovery also improved significantly in the HG (p &lt; 0.001). These findings confirm that short-duration HIIT is a highly effective strategy for enhancing aerobic capacity and metabolic efficiency in collegiate football players, and may be efficiently integrated into existing training schedules without compromising technical practice time..
