## Abstract
Abstract
                  
                    Sprinting ability is crucial in soccer, determining a player’s performance in key scenarios such as repeated high-speed changes of direction, pressing, and tracking back. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the effects of repeated sprint training (RST) on sprinting performance in youth soccer players. A systematic search was conducted across Chinese and English databases, including CNKI, PubMed, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, resulting in the inclusion of seven studies. The meta-analysis revealed that, compared to regular training, RST produced a significant improvement in RSA
                    mean
                    (
                    SMD
                    = -0.79, 95%
                    CI
                    : -1.40 to -0.18,

                     = 0.010) and short-distance sprint performance (
                    SMD
                    = -0.59, 95%
                    CI
                    : -0.92 to -0.26,
                    p
                     &lt; 0.001). In contrast, the effect of RST on RSA
                    best
                    (
                    SMD
                    = -0.08, 95%
                    CI
                    : -0.40 to 0.24,
                    p
                     = 0.60) was similar to that of regular training, with no significant difference. In conclusion, RST effectively enhances mean repeated sprint and short-distance sprint performance in youth soccer players. When implementing RST, factors such as the athlete’s age, fitness level, and training period should be taken into account.
